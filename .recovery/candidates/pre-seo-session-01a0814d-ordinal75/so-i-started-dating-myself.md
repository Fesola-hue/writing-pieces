I spent my teenage years trying to love myself, and each time I did, I failed.

I watched videos, read books, listened to audios and had a whole Spotify playlist of "bad bitch" and "boss bitch" songs.

None of it helped. I'd love myself for a few days and then the image would crash: no warning, no reason.

I kept thinking that if I recited the right things enough times, they would eventually stick. So I did. But I never actually got there. I just got good at pretending, until I didn't, because you can only hide that from yourself for so long.

After years of that, I stopped. And I started asking a different question: why did it seem to work for everyone except me?

The answer I landed on was that I wasn't actually trying to love myself. I was trying to perform it. I thought self-love was something you were born with and just had to unlock, like a cheat code. Manifest it hard enough and it appears.

That's not what it is.

The real problem was that my entire life was a book written by everyone around me. They picked things about me and wrote down how I should live, feel, eat, dress, go on about my days. The pen wasn't mine. The paper wasn't mine. Neither were the words.

So I was trying to love a version of myself I couldn't understand, because I hadn't written her. And that book never grew with me.

I wanted to write my own. So I got a blank page.

Starting on a blank page is hard. I felt empty sometimes, genuinely lost, but I didn't want to go back to the book that wasn't mine.

So, I decided to date myself.

I treated it like dating someone I barely knew: learning her through time and experience.

It was fascinating. I had a reason to be curious about myself. I'd write in my journal like, "hey, I just found out Hayyi loves milk, haha." Small things. But they were mine.

And somewhere in that, I realized loving myself was never meant to be linear. Never meant to be at 100%. I started making peace with the days I didn't feel like it.

Because it's not about loving yourself only on the good days. It's about being willing to be you when the storm hits too. Choosing yourself in the hard chapters. Writing different versions of your life, in your own words, with your own pen.

You can't manifest your way into that. The same way you never manifested your way into loving a person, you do it, you learn, you practise.
