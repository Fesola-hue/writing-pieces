I started working at 15 because the people I was around wanted to work after high school, and I just joined because of FOMO, even though I was insanely young.

I started dreaming of becoming a techie because I had a friend who would take a chunk of his time to send me links to different tech events, and through that, I could see a new possibility for myself.

My first salary was 20k and, as little as that was, it gave the next dream somewhere to start. I loved the freedom and elasticity that came with working for my own money.

I met my first gay friend on the internet at 16, and that changed how I saw everything around me.

I wanted to code because the people around me made it seem easy enough to try, and that got me thinking, "well, maybe I could give it a shot."

I started building a brand because I was around people who never made it seem impossible to do.

Somewhere along the way, I started forming my own opinions too. Knowing different people meant constantly being exposed to experiences outside my own.

Yet, I still get a lot of "wait, what?" whenever I tell people where I live.

And I think that's what got me thinking about all of this in the first place.

Maybe where you live isn't the only environment that raises you.

Sometimes it's the friend who keeps sending you links. The people who casually do something you once thought was difficult. Someone you met on the internet whose life makes you question something you've always believed. People who make an idea feel normal enough that one day you think, "well, maybe I could do that too."

A lot of the things I've done didn't start because I was particularly brave or ambitious. Most of the time, I was simply around someone who made another kind of life feel possible.

And I think there's something really beautiful about that.
